Alert sound is played by the alerts browser source, not by Mycelian's window. So the sound passes through several volume controls before it reaches your stream. Work along the chain below until you find the one that is off.

For file formats and folders, see Alert Media Configuration.

The audio chain

Quick check

  • Test Alert on the alert shows the image. (If not, the problem is not audio: see Troubleshooting Alerts.)
  • The sound card in the alert's Sound section shows a file name, and Volume is above 0%.
  • The Activity Feed button reads Mute, not a red Unmute, and the status pill does not say Ready · muted.
  • The browser source has Control audio via OBS on and shows in the OBS Audio Mixer.
  • The source is not muted in the mixer and its fader is up.
  • OBS's monitoring or your system volume lets you hear it.

Fix by symptom

Alerts are muted in Mycelian

Cause: Mute on the Activity Feed (or its OBS dock) silences every alert sound and text-to-speech. Images still show.

Fix: Click Unmute at the top of the Activity Feed.

The browser source is not in the Audio Mixer

Cause: Control audio via OBS is off, so OBS sends the source's sound straight to your desktop audio instead of the mixer.

Fix: In OBS, right-click the browser source → Properties, tick Control audio via OBS, click OK. The source now has its own mixer strip. If it does not appear, restart OBS.

No sound file, or the file moved

Cause: The sound card says No sound, or points at a file that was renamed or moved out of assets.

Fix: In the alert's Sound section, click Browse on the file card and pick the file again. Check it plays by opening its address in a browser, for example http://127.0.0.1:5000/assets/alerts/bits/cheer.mp3.

Random sounds never play

Cause: Random sounds is off, the Chance is 0, or the folder is not inside assets/alerts/. The log then shows Audio folder not found.

Fix: Move the folder under assets/alerts/, browse to it again, and set a chance above 0. See Alert Media.

It works in OBS but not in a browser tab

Cause: Normal browsers block sound on a page you have not clicked yet. OBS browser sources do not have this rule.

Fix: Click once anywhere on the overlay page, then test again.

Set levels in this order, testing after each:

  1. Per alert: the Volume slider in the alert's Sound section, then Test Alert.
  2. All alerts at once: the browser source's fader in the OBS Audio Mixer.
  3. Speech: TTS Volume in Source Settingsalerts.
The sound drops very low when an alert has TTS

Cause: This is on purpose. While text-to-speech is on, the alert sound plays at a quarter of its Volume so the voice is clear.

Fix: Raise the alert's Volume, or turn off Speak this alert for that alert.

Some files are much louder than others

Cause: The files were recorded at different levels. Random folders make this obvious.

Fix: Normalize every file to the same level (for example -12 to -6 dB peak) in an editor such as Audacity, then copy them back over the originals.

A starting point for Volume: follows 50-70%, subs 70-90%, big donations and raids 90-100%.

Crackling, pops or distortion

Causes and fixes, most likely first:

  • Clipping: the file is too loud. Lower the alert's Volume or normalize the file.
  • Sample rate mismatch: use 44.1 kHz or 48 kHz files, matching OBS's SettingsAudioSample Rate.
  • Damaged file: re-export or re-download it.
  • CPU load: close heavy programs, or see Performance Issues.
The sound starts or ends with a click

Fix: Set Fade in and Fade out to 20-100 ms in the alert's Sound section.

The sound is slightly behind the image

Cause: Browser audio can lag by 100-500 ms.

Fix: In OBS, open Advanced Audio Properties and give your other sources a matching Sync Offset, or accept the small lag.

The whole alert plays long after the event

Cause: Alerts play one at a time, and each waits for the previous sound to finish. Long sound files hold the queue.

Fix: Keep alert sounds to 3-10 seconds and trim silence at the end. Lower Delay Between Alerts in Source Settingsalerts. More in Troubleshooting Alerts.

An unexpected sound plays

Cause: A different alert matched (overlapping ranges), or the randomizer picked a file.

Fix: Click the event in the Activity Feed to replay it and see which alert it picks. Check the alert's Randomizer section; the Extra random sound roll comes first. See Configuring Alert Settings.

Sound plays twice

Cause: Two browser sources load the alerts page, for example a copy of the source in another scene, or the page open in a browser tab at the same time.

Fix: Keep one alerts browser source and add it to other scenes with Add Existing rather than a new copy.

Viewers hear alerts but you don't

Cause: OBS sends browser-source audio to the stream only, not to your headphones.

Fix: In the OBS Audio Mixer, open the source's menu → Advanced Audio Properties and set Audio Monitoring to Monitor and Output. Pick your headphones under SettingsAudioMonitoring Device.

You hear alerts but viewers don't

Cause: Monitoring is set to Monitor Only (mute output), or the source is excluded from your stream track.

Fix: Set Audio Monitoring to Monitor and Output, and check the source's track boxes in Advanced Audio Properties.

File checklist

FormatWorksNotes
MP3YesBest choice. 128-192 kbps keeps files small.
OGGYesSmall, open format.
WAVYesLarge files; fine for short sounds.
M4AYesFine in OBS.
FLACYesLossless and large. Convert to MP3 unless you need it.
AACRandom folders onlyThe Browse picker does not offer .aac for the primary sound.

Keep alert sounds 3-10 seconds and under about 2 MB. Longer files hold up the queue.

Music widgets

The Spotify widget only shows what is playing. The music itself comes from the Spotify app and never passes through Mycelian, so control its volume in Spotify or in OBS's desktop-audio source.
