Mycelian talks to Twitch through your own Twitch application. You create it once in the Twitch developer console, paste its Client ID and Client Secret into Mycelian, then sign in.

{{icon:schedule}} About 5 minutes {{icon:person}} Twitch broadcaster account {{icon:place}} SettingsTwitch

After the account is connected, see Twitch Integration for the events and switches that connection unlocks.

Before you start

  • You can sign in to Twitch as the channel owner (not a moderator account).
  • Two-factor authentication is turned on for that account. Twitch requires it to register applications.
  • Mycelian is open on this computer. The sign-in returns to http://localhost:17563, which only works on the machine running Mycelian.

Connect your account

Register a Twitch application

Open the Twitch developer console, sign in as the channel owner, and click Register Your Application. Fill in the form like this:

  1. Paste http://localhost:17563 exactly. It must be http (not https), localhost (not 127.0.0.1), port 17563, and no trailing slash.

Copy the Client ID and create a secret

Back on the application list, click Manage next to your new application.

  1. Copy the Client ID.
  2. Click New Secret, then copy the Client Secret right away. Twitch only shows it once.

Treat the Client Secret like a password. If you lose it or it leaks, click New Secret again and paste the new value into Mycelian. The old one stops working.

Paste both values into Mycelian

Open SettingsTwitch. On the Main account card, open Client ID & secret (it is already open until both values are saved) and paste the two values.

  1. Client ID & secret holds the credentials. The tag says Needed to connect until both are saved, then Saved, and the section starts folded. Both fields are masked; the eye icon shows the value.
  2. The Redirect URI box shows the address your Twitch application needs. Its copy icon copies it.
  3. Connect saves the Client ID and Client Secret and opens your browser. The {{icon:refresh}} icon beside it (Check status now) only re-reads the status and does not sign you in.
  4. Discard and Save sit in the tab's header. The Unsaved changes tag appears as soon as you edit something; Discard is only active while it shows.

Click Connect and approve access

Mycelian opens your browser on Twitch's authorization page. Sign in as the broadcaster and click Authorize. Accept every permission listed; Mycelian needs them for alerts, chat, polls, predictions and channel points.

The browser then shows a short confirmation page. Close it and return to Mycelian.

If no browser window opens, a pop-up blocker may have stopped it. Allow pop-ups for Mycelian and click Connect again. While Mycelian waits, the button reads Connecting.... If either credential field is empty you will see "Enter your Twitch Client ID and Client Secret first" instead.

Check the status

The Main account card should now look like this, with your channel name on the card and a green pill in the header:

The button now reads Reconnect. Mycelian refreshes the token on its own before it expires; Next refresh and Token expires count down on the card. You only need to click Reconnect if the card turns red. Connect already saved the credentials; if the header still shows Unsaved changes, click Save.

Optional: a separate chatbot account

Use the Chatbot account card when chat messages and announcements should come from a bot account instead of your channel.

Without one, the chatbot posts as your main account. The card then says Not connected with "Replies use your main account until you connect a bot", and the header shows Chatbot not connected. A dedicated bot shows the bot's name with "Connected · replies come from this account".

Reuse or create an application

On the Chatbot account card, open Client ID & secret and paste a Client ID and Client Secret. The same application as the main account works. A second application also works if its redirect URL is http://localhost:17563.

Sign out of the broadcaster in your browser

Twitch will not offer to switch accounts while the broadcaster is signed in. Sign out on twitch.tv, or copy the authorization link into a private window.

Connect the bot

Click Connect on the chatbot card and sign in as the bot account. The card should show the bot's name and Connected · replies come from this account, and the header pill turns to Chatbot connected.

Make the bot a moderator on your channel (/mod botname) so it can post announcements and run moderation actions.

Options on this tab

The switches are in the right-hand column, under Alerts and Raids. Click Save in the header after changing them.

SwitchWhat it does
Process Twitch alertsTurns Twitch events (follows, subs, bits, raids, channel points) into alerts. Turn it off to keep the connection but stop Twitch alerts.
Send a native Twitch shoutoutWhen someone raids you, Mycelian sends Twitch's built-in /shoutout for the raider.
Send a chat raid shoutoutWhen someone raids you, the chatbot posts a shoutout message with a link to the raider's channel in chat.

The Setup list under the switches repeats the steps on this page. A step gets a tick once Mycelian can see it is done (credentials saved, account connected).

Connection status

The bottom status bar uses these colors for Twitch. The full legend is in Status Footer.

StatusMeaning
ConnectedTwitch is active and receiving events
DegradedSigned in, but events have stopped or the session dropped
IdleNot set up yet, or waiting for you to sign in
ErrorThe connection failed. Check the log
No InternetThis computer cannot reach the network
UnreachableTwitch itself cannot be reached

If something goes wrong

Twitch says "redirect_mismatch"

The redirect URL in the developer console does not match exactly. Open your application with Manage, set the OAuth Redirect URL to http://localhost:17563, save, wait a minute, and click Connect again.

Twitch says "invalid client" or "invalid client secret"

The Client ID or Client Secret under Client ID & secret is wrong, has a stray space, or the secret was regenerated. Generate a new secret, paste both values again, and connect.

The browser never comes back to Mycelian

Something else on this computer is using port 17563, or a firewall is blocking it. Close other streaming tools that use Twitch sign-in, then try again. See Connection Problems.

The chatbot connected as my main account

The browser was still signed in as the broadcaster. Sign out of twitch.tv (or use a private window) and click Reconnect on the chatbot card.

The card says "Token expired", or the footer says Expired or Auth Failed

The token could not be refreshed, usually because the secret changed or access was revoked under Twitch → Settings → Connections. The card reads "Token expired · reconnect to sign in again". Click Reconnect to sign in again. If sign-in keeps failing, clear cookies for twitch.tv and retry.
